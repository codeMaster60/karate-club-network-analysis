Degree centrality

Popularity, counted plainly.

Counts the number of direct connections (edges) a node has. In directed networks, it splits into indegree (incoming ties / popularity) and outdegree (outgoing ties / activity).

Formula

CD(v) = deg(v) / (n − 1)
  • deg(v) number of friends member v has
  • n − 1 = 33 the most friends anyone could have, so the score runs 0 to 1

In plain words

How many people can this member talk to directly?

    Closeness centrality

    How fast word travels from you to everyone.

    Measures the average length of the shortest paths from a node to all other nodes in the network. High closeness means a node can quickly interact with or spread information to the entire network.

    Formula

    CC(v) = (n − 1) / Σu d(v, u)
    • d(v, u) fewest friendships linking v to u
    • Σ d(v, u) total steps to reach all 33 others
    • It is 1 ÷ the average distance, so closer means higher

    In plain words

    If this member starts a rumour, how quickly does the whole club hear it?

    c Core centrality metric c

    Betweenness centrality

    The gatekeepers every message must pass.

    Calculates how often a node falls on the shortest path between other pairs of nodes. Nodes with high scores act as vital bridges or gatekeepers controlling information flow between different clusters.

    Formula

    CB(v) = Σs ≠ v ≠ t σst(v) / σst
    • σst number of shortest paths from s to t
    • σst(v) how many of them pass through v
    • Added up over every pair, then divided by the 528 pairs that leave v out, giving 0 to 1

    In plain words

    When two members talk the shortest way, how often must it go through this one?

      Eigenvector centrality

      It is not how many you know. It is who.

      Assigns relative scores to all nodes based on the principle that connections to high-scoring (well-connected) nodes contribute more to the score of the node in question. PageRank is a famous variant of this concept.

      Formula

      xv = (1/λ) Σu ∈ N(v) xu  ⇔  A·x = λ·x
      • xv the score of member v
      • N(v) v's friends · A the adjacency matrix
      • λ its largest eigenvalue; the scores x are the matching eigenvector

      In plain words

      Are this member's friends important people?

      Click a member to follow them

        The famous variant

        PageRank

        Google's idea: a wanderer moves through the club. With probability d they follow a random friendship; otherwise they jump to anyone at random. A member's score is the share of time the wanderer spends with them. Each member splits its score evenly among its friends.

        PR(v) = (1 − d)/n + d · Σu ∈ N(v) PR(u) / deg(u)

        At d = 0 everyone ties at 1/34. Google's standard is 0.85. Drag it up and watch the hubs swell.

          Spearman rank correlation over all 34 members. 1.00 means two metrics put everyone in the same order.

          The Officer wins on popularity and influence. Mr. Hi wins on reach and control of the paths.

          Degree, eigenvector and PageRank crown the Officer; closeness and betweenness crown Mr. Hi. Degree and PageRank are almost the same ranking (0.98). Eigenvector differs most, because it rewards sitting inside the well-connected core, while betweenness rewards bridging between groups.

          Final scene · The verdict

          What the metrics told us

          0 & 33

          Every metric finds the two leaders

          From friendships alone, all five metrics put Mr. Hi and the Officer in their top two: the two men at the centre of the feud.

          44%

          Mr. Hi holds the club together

          He sits on 44% of all shortest paths. Remove him and 6 members lose all contact with the club. The Officer has more friends; Mr. Hi is the bridge.

          33/34

          The structure predicted the split

          "Join the nearer leader" gets 33 of 34 members right. Girvan–Newman, which cuts the busiest bridges, gets 32.

          29%

          A few carry everyone

          Take out the top 4 by betweenness and only 29% of the club stays connected. Take out 4 at random and about 86% does.
